---
title: "Make decisions because 99% are reversible"
excerpt: "Indecision is expensive. Most decisions can be corrected, adjusted, or reversed once you have better information."
---

This one sounds reckless to a lot of people.

They think every decision needs to be perfect. They overanalyse simple choices. They spend weeks researching things they could test in a day. They confuse thinking with progress. They wait for certainty in a world that never provides it.

I'm going to be extremely blunt here. Indecision is expensive. Every hour you spend trying to eliminate uncertainty is an hour you could have spent learning from reality. Most decisions can be corrected, adjusted, or reversed once you have better information. In a world that moves this fast, hesitation has a cost.

Perfect is the enemy of progress.

And as soon as you stop treating every decision like a life sentence, take action with the information you have, and adjust when reality proves you wrong… your life moves faster.

It's a pattern I can't ignore. The people who make progress don't wait for every variable to line up. They make small bets, gather feedback, and change direction when necessary. They save their deepest analysis for decisions that are genuinely difficult to reverse. Make the decision. Learn from the outcome. Keep moving. That's the real cheat code. Sorry.

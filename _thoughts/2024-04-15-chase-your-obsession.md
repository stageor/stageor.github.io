---
title: "Chase your obsession"
excerpt: "You cannot become exceptional at something you treat as a casual interest. Distraction is expensive."
---

This one scares a lot of people.

They think obsession is unhealthy. They tell you to find balance, keep your options open, and never get too attached to one thing. They jump between interests every few months. They confuse curiosity with commitment. They abandon things the moment progress gets uncomfortable.

I'm going to be extremely blunt here. You cannot become exceptional at something you treat as a casual interest. Mastery requires sustained attention, thousands of repetitions, and years of solving problems most people get bored with after a week. In a world where everyone is distracted, the ability to focus relentlessly on one thing is becoming a competitive advantage.

Distraction is expensive.

And as soon as you find something that genuinely pulls you in, commit to it beyond the point where motivation disappears, and keep going long enough to develop real expertise… you give yourself a chance to become exceptional.

It's a pattern I can't ignore. The people who build remarkable things don't spread their attention across a hundred competing priorities. They go deep. They follow questions nobody around them cares about. They stay with problems long after the novelty wears off. Find what consumes your curiosity. Protect your attention. Chase it until you become dangerous. That's the real cheat code. Sorry.

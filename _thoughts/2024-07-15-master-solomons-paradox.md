---
title: "Master Solomon's paradox"
excerpt: "You often have better judgment than your own decisions suggest. Distance changes everything."
---

This one exposes a lot of people.

They give brilliant advice to their friends, then make terrible decisions in their own lives. They can see every flaw in someone else's plan, spot every obvious mistake, and explain exactly what needs to change. Then the same problem lands on their own desk, and suddenly they can't think straight. They overanalyse. They hesitate. They let emotions make decisions that logic would never approve.

I'm going to be extremely blunt here. You often have better judgment than your own decisions suggest. Psychologists call this Solomon's paradox: people tend to reason more wisely about other people's problems than their own. When it's someone else's problem, you see the facts. When it's yours, your ego, fears, and emotions get involved.

Distance changes everything.

And as soon as you stop treating your own problems as something uniquely complicated, step back from the emotion, and examine your situation as if it belonged to someone else… the answer often becomes obvious.

It's a pattern I can't ignore. The smartest move isn't always finding a better answer. Sometimes it's creating enough distance to see the answer you already have. Stop asking what you feel like doing. Ask what you'd tell a friend to do. That's the real cheat code. Sorry.

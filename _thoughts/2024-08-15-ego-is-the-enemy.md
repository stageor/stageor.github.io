---
title: "Ego is the enemy"
excerpt: "Your ego is a tax on your potential. You don't know as much as you think."
---

This one destroys a lot of talented people.

They think they're smarter than everyone else. They defend their opinions like their identity depends on them. They reject feedback, avoid admitting mistakes, and mistake confidence for competence. They stop asking questions because they think they should already know the answers.

I'm going to be extremely blunt here. Your ego is a tax on your potential. The moment you believe you have nothing left to learn, you start falling behind. In a world where knowledge keeps expanding and entire industries change overnight, the person who thinks they know everything becomes the easiest person to replace.

You don't know as much as you think.

And as soon as you accept how much you don't know, become willing to look stupid while learning, and stop treating criticism as a personal attack… your rate of improvement changes.

It's a pattern I can't ignore. The people who keep getting better aren't necessarily the ones who start out the smartest. They're the ones who can admit they're wrong, absorb uncomfortable feedback, and change their minds when the evidence demands it. They ask questions their ego tells them they shouldn't ask. They learn from people they could easily dismiss. Kill your need to look impressive. Build the ability to improve. That's the real cheat code. Sorry.

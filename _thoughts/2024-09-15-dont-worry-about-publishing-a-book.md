---
title: "Don't worry about publishing a book"
excerpt: "A book is a terrible business model if nobody knows who you are. Writing is easy. Distribution is hard."
---

This one annoys a lot of aspiring authors.

They think writing a book will establish their authority, build their reputation, and generate passive income. They spend months polishing chapters, obsess over the perfect title, and imagine people discovering their work. Then they launch to an audience of twelve people and wonder why nothing happens.

I'm going to be extremely blunt here. A book is a terrible business model if nobody knows who you are. Most authors don't have a distribution problem with their writing. They have a distribution problem with their audience. In a world where publishing is easier than ever, getting attention is the actual competitive advantage.

Writing is easy. Distribution is hard.

And as soon as you stop treating a book as the business itself, build an audience that trusts your expertise, and use your writing to create opportunities beyond a one-time sale… the economics start to make sense.

It's a pattern I can't ignore. The people who make money from their knowledge understand that attention, trust, and access are often worth more than the information itself. They use free content to attract the right people, build relationships, and create opportunities for higher-value products and services. Don't spend a year writing a book nobody will read. Build a reason for people to read it first. That's the real cheat code. Sorry.

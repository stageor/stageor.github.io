---
title: "Plan in decades, execute in days"
excerpt: "Most people underestimate what they can accomplish in ten years and overestimate what they can accomplish in a week."
---

This one exposes a lot of people.

They think big goals require years of preparation. They spend months planning their next move, building elaborate roadmaps, and waiting for the perfect moment to start. They confuse having a vision with making progress. They think so far into the future that they forget to do anything today.

I'm going to be extremely blunt here. A decade is long enough to build something extraordinary, but only if you stop wasting the days that make it possible. Most people underestimate what they can accomplish in ten years and overestimate what they can accomplish in a week. They dream in decades but procrastinate in days.

Think bigger. Move faster.

And as soon as you develop a vision that stretches beyond your current capabilities, break it into immediate actions, and execute with an intensity that makes progress unavoidable… your future starts looking very different.

It's a pattern I can't ignore. The people who build remarkable things combine long-term patience with short-term urgency. They understand that meaningful results take years, but the next step rarely needs to take months. They don't need to see the entire staircase to climb the next step. Plan your life in decades. Plan your work in days. That's the real cheat code. Sorry.
